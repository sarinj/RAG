# Harborlight Regional Operations Manual

Manual version: 2.6
Status: approved
Effective date: 2026-09-01
Owner: Harborlight Regional Operations Office

## Current fares and transfers

The adult single fare is 2.40 Harborlight Credits.

The daily mobility pass costs 8.40 Harborlight Credits and is valid until the end of the service day on which it is first used.

The annual network pass costs 420 Harborlight Credits and covers city buses and trams for one calendar year.

One validated single fare allows transfers between city buses and trams for 75 minutes. The Airport Express service is excluded from this transfer rule.

Verified students and Harborlight residents aged 65 or older receive a 20 percent discount on monthly and annual network passes. The discount does not apply to single fares or daily passes.

Fare inspectors accept a digital receipt or a printed pass as proof of payment. A bank-card authorization alone is not proof that a fare was validated.

## Archived fare notice

The fare notice issued in 2024 allowed a 60-minute transfer between city buses and trams. That notice was superseded on 2026-09-01 by the current fares and transfers section of Manual version 2.6.

The archived notice listed the adult single fare as 2.25 Harborlight Credits. That amount is historical and must not be used for current fare questions.

## Route schedules and holidays

Route C7 runs from 05:30 until 23:15 on weekdays. Its Saturday service runs from 07:00 until 22:00.

Route C7 does not operate on designated public holidays, including Harbor Day.

Route D2 operates from 06:00 until 20:00 on designated public holidays. Its weekday schedule is 05:00 until 22:30.

Route R4 is an evening bus route. On weekdays it runs from 18:00 until 00:30, with its final departure leaving the Harbor Square terminal at 00:30.

The printed schedule for Route C7 is updated each March. Real-time service alerts take precedence when a temporary detour or cancellation is active.

## Accessibility and travel assistance

The Harborlight Access Desk arranges boarding assistance for passengers who need a ramp or a station escort. Requests should be submitted at least 48 hours before travel.

The Access Desk telephone number is 555-0148. It is staffed daily from 06:00 until 22:00.

If a station elevator is out of service, the control room must publish an accessibility alert and identify the nearest step-free alternate station. Staff must not promise that an elevator will be repaired by a particular time unless Facilities has confirmed the repair window.

Passengers may bring one mobility device without an additional fare. Oversized cargo bikes must use the marked accessible bay only when no wheelchair user is waiting to board.

## Rail signal operations

The signal reset checklist is labeled SIG-882. It applies only after the affected train has been removed from passenger service and the rail controller has authorized the reset.

The operator records the signal location, the displayed fault code, and the time of isolation before beginning SIG-882. A reset attempt without controller authorization is prohibited.

The separate SIG-828 checklist covers a failed platform display. It does not authorize work on trackside signals.

When two signal faults appear together, staff must report both codes to the rail controller rather than selecting the checklist with the closer-looking number.

## Fleet batteries and depot stock

Battery packs below 22 percent state of charge are removed from the electric-bus service rotation and sent to the depot charging bay.

Depot H-4 begins each morning shift with 18 fully charged spare battery packs. Packs are counted at shift handover; packs already assigned to a bus are not included in the spare count.

Depot H-7 begins each morning shift with 11 fully charged spare battery packs. Its charging equipment is not compatible with the H-4 battery model.

Fleet Control reviews the low-charge report at 04:30 each day. A pack may return to service only after its diagnostic test passes and the recorded state of charge is at least 80 percent.

Battery fault code BATT-204 means that a pack failed its temperature check. Code BATT-240 means that the charger did not complete a session; it does not by itself indicate a pack failure.

## Rail maintenance and wheel alarms

Preventive inspection for a passenger rail vehicle is due every 6,000 kilometres.

After a wheel assembly repair, the vehicle must complete a 30-minute controlled validation run before returning to passenger service.

A wheel-temperature alarm at or above 88 degrees Celsius requires the train to be removed from passenger service until a technician completes an inspection.

The maintenance log must include the vehicle number, the alarm reading, the inspection result, and the name of the technician who released the vehicle.

Door-sensor code DOOR-318 requires a platform-side inspection. It does not replace the wheel-temperature response.

## Heavy rain and flood response

Harborlight issues a flood watch when rainfall reaches 35 millimetres within any rolling three-hour period.

The flood watch instructs the Operations Desk to notify the rail controller, stage a track-inspection crew, and monitor the underpass gauges every 15 minutes.

An inspection crew must arrive at the assigned underpass within 25 minutes after an alert is issued.

Rail service through an underpass must be suspended when standing water reaches 15 centimetres above the marked rail-bed reference line. The controller may suspend service sooner if debris or current creates an immediate hazard.

The crew records the gauge reading and a timestamp before requesting that service resume. A falling water level alone is not approval to reopen the route.

## Purchasing and vendor selection

For a purchase below 25,000 Harborlight Credits, the requesting team must attach one written price quote and a short business justification.

For a purchase from 25,000 through 99,999 Harborlight Credits, the team must obtain three written quotes and record the selection reason.

A purchase of 100,000 Harborlight Credits or more requires an open competitive tender administered by Procurement.

The value is calculated across the full contract term, including planned extensions. Dividing one planned purchase into smaller orders to avoid a threshold is prohibited.

An emergency purchase may use the emergency exception only when the Operations Director records the immediate risk and Procurement logs the exception within two business days.

## Water quality response

Water-quality procedure WQ-31 applies when a treatment-plant turbidity reading exceeds 0.8 NTU.

The operator must take a confirmatory sample, notify the shift supervisor, and pause discharge from the affected treatment line until the confirmatory result is reviewed.

The separate WQ-13 procedure covers a low chlorine residual. It does not replace WQ-31 when a turbidity reading is above its limit.

Operators record the sample time, instrument identifier, reading, and supervisor notification in the plant log. A verbal notification does not replace the written log entry.

## Fire and emergency command

Emergency checklist FIRE-27 requires staff to evacuate the affected public area and call emergency services when smoke or an uncontrolled flame is reported inside a station.

The station supervisor then reports the station name, affected entrance, and evacuation status to the Operations Desk. Staff must not re-enter until emergency services authorize access.

FIRE-72 is the checklist for a fire-alarm panel fault with no report of smoke or flame. It requires a technician call and a station announcement, but it does not direct a full evacuation by itself.

## Data handling and camera recordings

Station camera recordings are retained for 30 days unless an incident hold is opened. An incident hold preserves the relevant recording until the case owner releases it.

Routine service dashboards must not display passenger names, payment-card numbers, or unmasked contact details.

Operational event logs are retained for 365 days. This retention period does not authorize staff to export passenger-level records to personal devices.

## Lost property and service desk

Lost property is held at the Harbor Square Service Desk for 45 calendar days. Unclaimed items are then transferred to the municipal property office for disposal or donation review.

The city service desk can be reached at 311 for general transit questions. It cannot approve refunds while a fare dispute is under review.

## Staff communications and service alerts

The Operations Desk publishes a service alert after a route closure is confirmed by the responsible controller. Draft alerts must be labeled unconfirmed and are not sent to passenger channels.

For a planned weekend maintenance closure, Communications should receive the approved route impact at least five calendar days before the closure begins.

An urgent safety closure has no five-day notice requirement. The controller issues the closure immediately and Communications publishes the confirmed alert as soon as the route details are verified.

## Document precedence

Manual version 2.6 is the approved source for current Harborlight operating procedures as of 2026-09-01. Archived notices remain available for historical questions but do not override an active section of version 2.6.

When this manual gives a numeric threshold and a local checklist appears to differ, staff must follow the active manual and ask the responsible supervisor to resolve the discrepancy before acting.
